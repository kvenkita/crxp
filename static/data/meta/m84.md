## Jobs–housing balance
Workplace jobs per household (LEHD LODES WAC over ACS occupied units)

### Why is this important?
The ratio of jobs to households distinguishes employment centers (>1) from bedroom communities (<1), a core measure of local land-use balance. Higher and lower values are not inherently better or worse.

### About the Data
Workplace jobs from the Census Bureau's LEHD Origin-Destination Employment Statistics (LODES8 Workplace Area Characteristics, 2014–2024), summed from 2020 census blocks to tracts. LODES is a synthetic, noise-infused full count rather than a survey sample, so it carries no sampling margin of error. The denominator is occupied housing units from the American Community Survey, which does carry a margin of error that this ratio does not yet propagate. A few tracts hold almost no housing — an airport, a park, an industrial zone — where jobs per household runs into the hundreds and describes the absence of housing rather than a jobs–housing balance. Those tracts are left blank: the ratio is published only where a tract has at least 50 households.

_**Source**: U.S. Census Bureau, LEHD Origin-Destination Employment Statistics (LODES)._
